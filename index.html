How to play

Be the last player with dice. Two to six players, five dice each. Everyone rolls in secret and only sees their own dice.

Who starts

  • When the host starts the game, every player rolls one die. The highest roll starts; tied players roll again.
  • Play goes around the table in seat order, the order people joined.

Your turn

  • Either raise the bid or call Dudo (“I doubt it”).
  • A bid is a quantity and a face, like five 4s: a claim about all the dice on the table, yours included.
  • 1s are wild: they count as whatever face is bid. A round cannot open on 1s.
  • To raise, bid more dice of any face, or the same number of dice with a higher face. You may jump higher than needed.
  • Switching to 1s: at least half the current quantity, rounded up (seven 4s → four 1s).
  • Switching away from 1s: at least double plus one (four 1s → nine 2s).
  • A bid can’t name more dice than are on the table.

Dudo

  • Everyone’s dice are revealed and the matching dice plus wild 1s are counted.
  • Fewer than the bid: the bidder loses a die. Equal or more: the caller loses a die.
  • Whoever lost a die opens the next round. A player with no dice is out, and the next player opens instead.

Calza (optional, chosen when the table is made)

  • Any player except the one who made the bid can call Calza at any time: “the bid is exactly right.”
  • Exactly right: the caller wins a die back, up to five. Otherwise the caller loses a die. The bidder never loses a die to Calza.
  • The caller opens the next round.

Palifico

  • The first time a player drops to their last die, the next round is a palifico round, opened by that player. It happens once per player.
  • In palifico, 1s are not wild. The opener names any face, 1s included, and that face is locked for the round: players can only raise the quantity.

On this page

  • Whoever creates the table hosts it: their phone runs the game, so it needs to keep this page open with the screen on.
  • Friends join by scanning the host’s QR code, opening the shared link, or typing the five-letter table code.
  • Your phone rolls your dice and keeps them to itself until someone calls Dudo or Calza. Tap your cup to show or hide them.
  • Phones talk to each other directly, which works best on the same wifi.
  • If someone leaves mid-game, the round restarts with fresh dice. The host can remove a player whose phone has gone quiet.
  • On a new phone? Open the table and take over your seat.
